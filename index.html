      How CalcInk works

      Writing

      • Write like on paper and end with = (e.g. 18 + 4 × 3 =) - the answer appears to the right of the equals sign.
      • Start with = instead (= 17 × 8) to get the answer on the left.
      • Write an answer yourself (2 + 2 = 4) and CalcInk checks it with ✓ or ✗.
      • Edit freely: erase a digit or write a new one and the answer updates. Division by zero shows Undefined; a ? after an answer means the model was unsure - rewrite that symbol.
      • Scribble back and forth over ink to erase it. Ink colour doesn't matter: 2 + 3 = in different colours is read the same.

      The model

      Handwriting is recognised entirely in your browser - no cloud APIs, and it works offline once loaded. Nothing was trained from scratch; two existing open-source models run in a Web Worker so drawing stays at 60 fps:

      • Symbol classifier - a 16-class CNN (digits, + − × ÷ . =) from Math_Symbols_Classify (MIT), converted to ONNX.
      • MNIST digit CNN - from the ONNX Model Zoo; decides which digit, averaged over 9 renderings of the same ink.
      • A shape prior (stroke count, flatness, size) breaks ties, e.g. "−" is one flat stroke and "=" is two.

      Strokes are split into rows, then symbols, then the recognised text is evaluated by a safe BODMAS/PEMDAS parser (no eval) that handles decimals and negative numbers.
